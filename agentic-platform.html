Reference architecture · The lean backend behind Basil Meridian

A future-proof agentic platform.

Our whole model is asset-light: a small, dedicated team delivering the service of a much larger one. That only works if the backend does the heavy lifting. This is how we think about that backend — a composable, vendor-neutral agentic mesh that lets agents reason, collaborate, and act across our sourcing, compliance, and logistics work, securely and at scale, and built to keep evolving as the technology does.

01The thesis

Point tools don't scale. A mesh does.

The instinct with AI is to bolt a chatbot onto each task: one for quoting, one for documents, one for supplier chase-ups. It works for a demo and collapses in production — brittle integrations, no shared context, no way to govern what a dozen disconnected bots actually do.

A future-proof platform inverts that. Instead of point solutions, it provides a connective and orchestration layer — a mesh — where many agents (ours and off-the-shelf) share context, delegate tasks to one another, and act across every system we touch. The mesh, not any single model or vendor, is the durable asset. Models and tools underneath it are meant to be swapped as the field moves; the architecture is designed so that swap costs almost nothing.

🧩

Composable

Every capability — an agent, an evaluator, a connector, a model policy — is a reusable building block that snaps in and out without reworking the system around it. New need, new block; no rewrite.

🌿

Compostable

Just as important: any component can be retired cleanly when a better one appears. Nothing calcifies into lock-in. Yesterday's best model decomposes out; today's takes its place — same interfaces, no drama.

02Design principles

Five principles that keep it future-proof

These are mutually reinforcing. Drop one and the platform quietly becomes another pile of brittle integrations. Together they define the agentic AI mesh.

01

Composability

Integrate any agent or tool without reworking the wider system. Capabilities plug in through shared interfaces.

02

Distributed intelligence

Break a job into steps handled by a network of cooperating agents, rather than one monolith trying to do everything.

03

Layered decoupling

Keep logic, memory, and orchestration in separate layers so each can change independently.

04

Vendor neutrality

Update or replace any component through open standards — no dependence on a single provider's roadmap.

05

Governed autonomy

Embed policy, guardrails, and audit directly in the mesh so agents act freely but safely and transparently.

A composable, layered architecture

04Operating model

Two structural layers, working together

Scale needs more than good architecture — it needs a way of working. A platform team builds and maintains reusable components; an operations side composes them into real workflows. Standard interfaces connect the two, so quality and control never become bottlenecks.

Platform side

builds & maintains reusable components
  • Curates agents, evaluators, and model policies as versioned building blocks
  • Owns the shared interfaces, standardised nodes, and MCP contracts
  • Sets guardrails, observability, and the evaluation harness once, for everyone
  • Keeps the mesh vendor-neutral so models and tools stay swappable

Operations side

composes components into workflows
  • Assembles workflows in visual tools — n8n, Power Automate, agent builders
  • Consumes platform components through APIs and standardised nodes
  • Moves fast on new sourcing, compliance, and logistics flows without waiting on engineering
  • Inherits quality, observability, and control by construction — not by policing
05Governed autonomy

Autonomy you can actually trust

Agents that touch customs paperwork, prices, and shipments cannot be a black box. Governance is a first-class layer of the mesh — the thing that makes autonomy safe enough to expand.

1

Identity & least privilege

Every agent has an identity and only the access its task requires. Nothing acts on our behalf anonymously.

2

Guardrails & approvals

Policies constrain what agents may do; high-stakes actions — a price commit, a customs filing — route to a human check.

3

Observability & tracing

Every step an agent takes is traced end to end, so behaviour is explainable and debuggable, not guessed at.

4

Continuous evaluation

Agents are scored against known-good outcomes before and after they ship, so quality is measured, not assumed.

5

Audit & reproducibility

A durable record of who did what, with which inputs — the same discipline our compliance work already lives by.

6

Cost & rate control

Budgets and limits per workflow keep autonomous work economical as it scales across the business.

06Applied

What the mesh does for a sourcing & supply firm

These are the workflows that let a small team behave like a large one. Each is a set of cooperating agents drawing on shared tools and context — not a standalone bot.

sourcing + pricing
Demand-first sourcing

Agents translate a buyer's brief into a shortlist of vetted Thai suppliers, pull specs, and assemble an indicative landed price — with a human confirming the quote.

compliance
Market-ready documentation

A compliance agent drafts labels, certificates, and customs paperwork for the destination market, checked against current rules before anything ships.

logistics
Consolidation & tracking

Logistics agents plan mixed-pallet consolidation, book freight, and keep buyers posted at every stage without anyone chasing a spreadsheet.

accounts
Reorder & sell-through support

Agents watch sell-through signals, surface reorder moments, and prep the next order so buying again is effortless.

Build the mesh. Rent the models.

The durable investment is the architecture and the governance around it. The models, tools, and vendors underneath are meant to be swapped — and a future-proof platform makes that swap cheap by design.

07Roadmap

How you get there without a big-bang rebuild

Future-proof does not mean build everything now. It means each step is composable, so nothing you build has to be thrown away when the next capability arrives.

1
foundation

Standards first

Adopt open interfaces (MCP), identity, and tracing before adding agents. The plumbing is the platform.

2
first agents

One workflow, end to end

Ship a single high-value flow — say, documentation — with governance on from day one.

3
the mesh

Let agents collaborate

Add orchestration so agents delegate and share context, and let ops compose new flows in visual tools.

4
scale

Compose, measure, swap

Grow the component library, evaluate continuously, and retire components cleanly as better ones appear.
